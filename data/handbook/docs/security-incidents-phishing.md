# Security incidents and phishing
Department: Security
Updated: 2026-02-02

## What a security incident is

A security incident is anything that could harm the safety of our data, systems or people. Examples are a lost or stolen device, data sent to the wrong person, a suspicious sign-in alert, a password you think someone knows, malware, a stranger in the office without a badge, and an AI tool that follows instructions hidden in a document.

## Report within 1 hour

Report every security incident within 1 hour of noticing it. Email security@fernhill.example, or call extension 4499. Out of hours, the call goes to the on-call security engineer. You will never be blamed for reporting quickly, even if the incident was your own mistake. Hiding an incident is treated far more seriously than causing one.

## Phishing

Phishing is a message that tries to trick you into clicking a link, opening a file, sharing a password or paying money. Signs of phishing are urgency, a sender address that is slightly wrong, a request to bypass normal steps, and a link whose address does not match the text.

## What to do with a suspicious email

- Do not click links or open attachments.
- Use the Report phishing button in your email app. It sends the message to the Security team and removes it from your inbox.
- Do not forward the email to colleagues to warn them; the Security team does that.
- If you already clicked or entered a password, report it at once and change the password.

## Payment requests

Finance never changes a supplier's bank details because of an email alone. Any request to change bank details, or to make an urgent payment, is checked by a phone call to a known number. If a senior leader asks you by message to buy gift cards or make a payment, it is almost certainly a scam: report it.

## Phishing tests

The Security team sends harmless test phishing emails every quarter. If you click one, you get a short training video, and nobody else is told.

## After an incident

The Security team may ask you for details, your device or a short meeting. Within 5 working days of a serious incident, the team writes a short review of what happened and what will change, without blame.
