# Expense claims
Department: Finance
Updated: 2026-01-12

## What you can claim

The company pays back reasonable costs that you pay yourself for work. Examples are local taxis, visa fees, conference tickets approved from your learning budget, and the daily meal allowance on trips. Flights, trains and hotels booked through TripDesk are already paid by the company, so never claim them.

## Deadlines

Submit each claim in ExpenseHub within 30 days of spending the money. Claims more than 90 days old are not paid at all, whatever the reason. Submit claims at least once a month, rather than saving them up.

## Receipts

Attach a receipt for every item over ₹500. A clear photo of the receipt is fine, and you do not need to keep the paper. The receipt must show the date, the seller and the amount. The daily meal allowance on trips needs no receipts. If you lose a receipt, fill in a missing-receipt declaration in ExpenseHub. You may do this at most twice a year.

## Approval

Your line manager approves your claims in ExpenseHub. Managers check that each cost was for work, that it follows the policies, and that a receipt is attached where needed. If your manager is on leave, ExpenseHub sends the claim to their manager after 5 working days. Claims over ₹50,000 are also checked by Finance before payment.

## When you are paid

Claims approved by the 20th of the month are paid with that month's salary, on the last working day of the month. Claims approved after the 20th are paid with the next month's salary. The payment appears as a separate line on your payslip.

## What we do not pay

- Alcohol, except as part of client entertainment.
- Fines and penalties of any kind.
- Personal items, and gifts for colleagues.
- Upgrades you choose yourself, such as a better seat or room.
- Anything booked through TripDesk.
- Food and drink while you work at home or in your own office, except the late-working meal.

## Getting it wrong

A claim that breaks the rules is returned to you to correct. Claiming a cost twice, or claiming a personal cost as a business cost, is treated as misconduct.
