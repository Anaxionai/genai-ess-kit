# Data classification
Department: Security
Updated: 2026-02-02

## The four levels

Every document, file and dataset at Fernhill Software belongs to one of four levels. The level decides where it may be stored and who may see it.

- Public: information we publish or could publish without harm, such as the website, product brochures and job adverts.
- Internal: everyday company information for employees only, such as this handbook, team plans and meeting notes.
- Confidential: information that would harm the company or a customer if it leaked, such as contracts, product roadmaps, source code and financial results before they are published.
- Restricted: the most sensitive information, such as salary data, customer personal data, payment card data, security keys and passwords, and medical information about employees.

## Labelling

Label Confidential and Restricted documents in their title or first line. Anything without a label is treated as Internal. If you are not sure of the level, choose the higher one and ask the Security team.

## Rules for each level

- Public data may be shared with anyone.
- Internal data may be shared with employees and with contractors who need it.
- Confidential data may be shared outside the company only with a signed non-disclosure agreement, and only through company tools.
- Restricted data may be stored only in approved systems. It may never be sent outside the company by email, never sent to a personal email address, never stored on a USB drive, and never given to any AI tool.

## Sharing files

Share files with links from company cloud storage, not as email attachments. Set links to named people, not to anyone with the link. Remove external access when a project ends.

## Printing and paper

Print Confidential and Restricted documents only when you must. Collect printouts at once and shred them in the locked bins when you no longer need them.

## Keeping and deleting data

Keep data only as long as there is a reason. Customer personal data is deleted 12 months after a customer leaves, unless the law requires longer.

## Mistakes

If you send data to the wrong person or share it at the wrong level, report it within 1 hour as a security incident. Quick reports make most mistakes harmless.
