# Code of conduct
Department: People
Updated: 2026-01-05

## How we treat each other

Treat colleagues, customers and partners with respect. Harassment, bullying and discrimination of any kind are not tolerated, in the office, online, or at work events. Disagree with ideas, not with people. If someone tells you that your words or actions upset them, listen and apologise.

## Gifts and hospitality

You may keep a gift from a customer, supplier or partner if it is worth up to ₹2,500. Gifts worth more than ₹2,500 must be declared in the gifts register in PeopleHub within 5 working days, and are usually returned politely. Never accept cash, vouchers or gift cards, whatever their value. Invitations to meals or events with suppliers are fine if they are occasional, of modest value and have a clear business reason. Decline any gift or invitation during a purchase decision that involves the giver.

## Conflicts of interest

A conflict of interest arises when your private interests could affect your work decisions. Declare it to the People team in PeopleHub. Examples are a second job, a stake in a supplier or competitor, a family member who works for a supplier, or a close relationship with someone you manage. Declaring a conflict is not wrongdoing; hiding one is.

## Second jobs

You may have a second job or a business of your own if it does not compete with Fernhill Software, does not use company time or equipment, and does not affect your work. Declare it before you start it.

## Company property and information

Use company equipment and money only for company purposes, as the other policies describe. Keep Confidential and Restricted information safe, also after you leave the company.

## Speaking up

If you see something that breaks the law or this code, speak up. Tell your manager, the People team, or use the ethics hotline. The hotline is run by an independent provider and lets you report anonymously, by phone or online, 24 hours a day. Every report is investigated. Nobody will be punished for raising a concern in good faith, and retaliation against someone who speaks up is itself a serious breach of this code.

## Breaking this code

Breaking this code may lead to disciplinary action, up to dismissal. Serious breaches, such as fraud or harassment, may also be reported to the police.
